# Vaccination Outreach Feedback Analysis

## What Happens After People Show Up?

This repository presents an analysis of beneficiary and staff feedback from vaccination outreach across five states. Using Excel pivot tables, records from 500 beneficiaries and 50 field staff were analysed to show what attendees experienced, what staff saw on the ground, and where the two views agree or differ. The findings are summarised in a two-page, plain-language brief for programme teams.

This project uses synthetic practice data. No real people or programme results are involved.

## About the Data

* 500 beneficiary records and 50 staff records across five states (synthetic).
* Because the data is synthetic, the insights below show how the method works. They are not findings about any real programme.
* The analysis covers people who attended. It does not cover people who stayed home, which is addressed in the follow-up note linked below.

## Key Insights (Illustrative)

* 67.4% of beneficiaries said something made it difficult to come. The most common barriers were lack of information, distance or transport cost, and work or household responsibilities.
* The average wait was 46 minutes. About one in five people waited more than an hour and rated their visit 2.8 out of 5, against 3.8 for those who waited 30 minutes or less. Longer waits were linked with lower satisfaction, but the data does not show that waiting caused it.
* The most common requests were shorter waiting times (29%), more outreach days (21%), clinics closer to home (18%) and more information before outreach (17%).
* Staff rated community awareness work lowest in Katsina and Lagos. Those are also the two states where people most often named lack of information as their biggest barrier, about 1 in 4 compared with roughly 1 in 8 to 1 in 6 elsewhere.
* Kano had the highest average wait (about 50 minutes), yet only one of its 14 staff records named long waiting time as the main challenge. Staff and beneficiaries do not always experience a queue the same way.

## Tool Used

Microsoft Excel: pivot tables and summary tables.

## Methodology

* **Data checks:** Records were checked and cleaned before analysis, and every figure in the brief was checked against the raw data.
* **Pivot summaries:** Pivot tables summarised barriers, waiting time, satisfaction, information channels, language and requests, overall and by state.
* **Comparing two views:** Beneficiary feedback was set beside staff-reported mobilisation and operational challenges at state level.
* **Cautious wording:** Findings are described as links, not causes, and the brief states what the data does and does not show.

## Repository Structure

* **Brief:** Two-page PDF for programme teams.
* **Data and Analysis (Excel Workbooks):**
   * Beneficiary survey, pivot summaries, statistics, and correlation
   * Staff survey, pivot summaries
